## Course identity and scope

- **Course:** Research Paper Writing Seminar I (RPW1)
- **Institution:** University of Aizu
- **Academic year and term:** AY2026, S2 (Q3 and Q4)
- **Focus:** Scientific writing for computer science and engineering, from reading related work to drafting, reviewing, and revising a paper.
- **Scope:** Students' supervisors guide their research. RPW1 develops research-writing skills; general English study is separate.

Examples should work across fields such as systems, networks, security, software, databases, algorithms, AI, graphics, and human–computer interaction. Explain field-specific terminology before using it to teach a writing principle.

## Learning goals

Students should learn to:

- Understand the structure and purpose of research-paper sections.
- Explain a focused research question and a clear contribution.
- Connect claims to methods, evidence, and limitations.
- Read and compare related work and cite sources accurately.
- Present technical content and results clearly.
- Apply ethical writing and submission practices.
- Give useful peer reviews and revise a draft in response to feedback.

## Teaching approach

The current lecture emphasizes these principles:

- **Research serves a purpose beyond publication.** Papers communicate discoveries that can advance knowledge and benefit society. Emphasize quality rather than publication counts.
- **Precision and simple language matter.** Help readers understand the problem, contribution, and evidence. Avoid impressive wording that obscures meaning.
- **Writing is part of reasoning.** Use writing to test whether the research question, method, evidence, and conclusion fit together.
- **Examples need clear boundaries.** Label hypothetical scenarios and illustrative numbers. Do not present them as measured results or claim a research gap without evidence.
- **Revision is a normal part of writing.** Address content and structure before polishing sentences. Ask readers to explain the main point back to the author.
- **Peer review should be constructive.** Teach specific, fair comments and respectful responses supported by reasons and evidence.

Keep each slide focused on one main idea. Use diagrams and concrete examples when they clarify a process or argument. Put fuller explanations, discussion prompts, and source notes in speaker notes. Use short questions and activities to connect the lesson to students' own research.

## Assignments and assessment

The current Session 01 deck describes this assignment flow:

1. Each student writes an individual paper draft.
2. Three students independently review each draft at midterm.
3. The author revises the draft for final submission, addressing concerns from all three reviewers.
4. The author explains the changes made. Disagreements require reasons and supporting evidence.
5. Three students review each final draft in the final review round.
6. Instructors grade both the paper drafts and the student reviews.

The deck records a **50% class activities / 50% paper draft** grading split. Class activities include peer reviews; the paper draft includes midterm and final rounds. Detailed rubrics, deadlines, submission formats, attendance requirements, and permitted help must come from current ELMS announcements. Do not invent additional assessment rules.
