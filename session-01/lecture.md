# Citations and plagiarism

![w:1100 h:310](images/diagrams/citations.svg)

- Cite your own previous work when you reuse or extend it. 
- Be cautious of AI hallucinations, and verify AI-generated claims and references.
- Permission may be needed for reproducing.

<!--
Presenter notes

Give credit when you use another person’s words or work.

- Cite ideas, results, figures, data, and code that you use.
- Mark exact wording as a quotation and cite it.
- Use your own wording when you paraphrase, and still cite the source.
- Cite earlier work of your own when you reuse or extend it.

Check permissions and publication rules when reusing material.

# Common problems in a draft



- A broad problem with no clear research question
- A contribution that readers cannot identify
- Claims without enough evidence
- A list of papers without comparison or analysis
- Figures or explanations that are hard to follow
- Missing citations or incorrect formatting

Fix the main argument before polishing individual sentences.

# Peer review


Peer review asks other researchers to assess a paper.

Reviewers examine:

- The question and contribution
- The technical approach
- The evidence and conclusions
- The clarity of the explanation

The editor or program committee makes the decision.

Source: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/submit-your-article-for-peer-review/about-the-peer-review-process/ (checked 2026-10-01).

# Choosing a publication venue



Discuss the choice with your supervisor.

- Does the venue publish work on your topic?
- Who is the intended audience?
- Are the review process and publisher clear?
- Can you meet the format and deadline?
- Are publication costs and policies acceptable?

Use rankings as one source of information.
Read recent papers and assess their relevance.
